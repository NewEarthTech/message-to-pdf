Frequently asked questions

Why does Sheaf need Full Disk Access?

macOS treats your Messages database as private — even you can't read it without permission. Full Disk Access is the switch Apple provides to grant that permission. Sheaf uses it only to read your messages on your Mac so it can build the PDF. It doesn't upload anything, and you can revoke the access any time in System Settings.

Is it actually private?

Yes. Sheaf runs entirely on your Mac. It has no account, no analytics, no telemetry, and makes no network calls of its own — your conversations never leave your computer. The only data that goes anywhere is what you type into checkout, which is handled by our reseller, Paddle. And because Sheaf is open source, you don't have to take our word for any of this — the code is public.

Is Sheaf open source? Where's the code?

Sheaf is free software under the GNU GPL v3. You can read every line, audit it, or build it yourself. The source lives on GitHub. Paying for Sheaf gets you the signed, notarized, ready-to-run build and supports the work.

If it's open source, why pay?

You're paying for the finished thing: a build that's signed and notarized by Apple so it opens with a double-click, kept up to date, and made by someone you can email. It's the same reason people buy software they could technically compile themselves — convenience, trust, and supporting the maker.

What does it export, exactly?

A PDF that mirrors the conversation: message bubbles in reading order, photos and image attachments inline, contact names, and timestamps. It handles both iMessage (blue) and SMS (green). You can export the full history or narrow it to a date range.

Does it do group chats?

Not yet — Sheaf currently exports one-on-one conversations. Group chats are on the list.

Which Macs are supported?

Sheaf is a universal app: it runs natively on both Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, on macOS 11 (Big Sur) and later.
